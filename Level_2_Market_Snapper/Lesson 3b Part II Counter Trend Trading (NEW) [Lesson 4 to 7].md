## 1. Introduction to Counter Trend Trading

Welcome to Part 2 of the course. Congratulations for making it this far. Part 2 focuses on learning a distinct trading approach known as **Counter Trend Trading**, also referred to as **Mean Reversion Trading**.

### 1.1 Definition and Core Objective
As the name suggests, counter trend trading involves trading against the prevailing trend. The primary objective is to catch reversals in the trend by:
*   **Buying** at the bottom of a downtrend.
*   **Selling** at the top of an uptrend.

This approach seeks to capitalize on price movements that have become overextended, anticipating a return to equilibrium or a change in direction.

## 2. Selling at the Top of an Uptrend

A fundamental component of counter trend trading is identifying opportunities to sell short at the peak of an uptrend.

### 2.1 Market Structure and Overbought Conditions
To understand when to sell, one must analyze the structure of an uptrend. A typical uptrend consists of a repeating pattern:
1.  Impulsive wave up.
2.  Corrective wave down.
3.  Impulsive wave up.
4.  Corrective wave down.

Following this sequence, the price may continue upward but becomes overstretched or **overbought**. At this stage, there is a high probability that the price will reverse back down.

### 2.2 Potential Outcomes of a Reversal
When an uptrend becomes overextended, the reversal may result in one of two scenarios:
*   The price reverses back down to the support level.
*   The price drops significantly, potentially changing the trend entirely.

While the exact outcome cannot be predicted with certainty, the goal of this approach is to sell short right at the top of the uptrend.

### 2.3 Identifying the Top: Tools and Techniques
Picking the exact top of an uptrend is challenging. However, specific techniques can assist in identifying these reversal points:
*   **Oscillators:** Used to gauge momentum and overbought conditions.
*   **Divergence:** A technique detailed in Lesson 4.

#### 2.3.1 Understanding Divergence in Uptrends
Divergence serves as an early warning signal and a leading indicator that a price reversal is likely. In the context of an uptrend, divergence occurs when:
*   The **price** is going up.
*   One of the **indicators** is going down.

This discrepancy creates a divergence, signaling that the upward momentum is weakening and the price is likely to start reversing down.

### 2.4 Trade Execution
Upon identifying a divergence or overbought condition at the top of an uptrend:
1.  **Entry:** Sell short at the top of the uptrend.
2.  **Exit:** Take profit at a predetermined level as the price moves down.

## 3. Buying at the Bottom of a Downtrend

Counter trend trading also involves buying at the bottom of a downtrend just as it reverses back up.

### 3.1 Market Structure and Oversold Conditions
Similar to uptrends, downtrends follow a structural pattern:
1.  Impulsive wave down.
2.  Corrective wave up.
3.  Impulsive wave down.
4.  Corrective wave up.

Occasionally, the price drops rapidly, becoming overextended. When this occurs, there is a high chance the price will reverse back up.

### 3.2 Potential Outcomes of a Reversal
When a downtrend becomes overextended, the reversal may result in:
*   The price reversing back up to the top of the downtrend.
*   The price continuing upward, potentially changing into a new uptrend.

The objective is to buy and go long right at the bottom of the downtrend to catch a quick swing trade upward and take profit at a higher level.

### 3.3 Identifying the Bottom: Tools and Techniques
Determining when a downtrend is ready to reverse relies on specific technical tools:
*   **Oscillators:** These indicators signal when the market is **oversold**.
*   **Divergence:** A pattern detailed in Lesson 4.

#### 3.3.1 Understanding Divergence in Downtrends
In the context of a downtrend, divergence acts as a leading indicator that the price is likely to reverse back up. This pattern is identified when:
*   The **price** is going down.
*   One of the **indicators** is going up.

This divergence indicates weakening downward momentum and suggests an impending reversal.
