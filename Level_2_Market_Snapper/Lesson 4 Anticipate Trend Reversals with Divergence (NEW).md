## 4.1 Introduction to Divergence

This chapter introduces the foundational concept required for counter-trend trading strategies, specifically the Bollinger Mean Reversion and Capitulation strategies. Before executing these strategies, traders must understand how to anticipate price reversals using **divergence**.

### 4.1.1 Definition and Core Concept
To diverge means to move in opposite directions. In technical analysis, divergence occurs when there is a conflict between the price trend and the trend of a technical indicator. This misalignment serves as a leading signal that the current price trend is losing momentum and a reversal is probable.

Traders can identify divergence using various indicators, including:
*   MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence)
*   Stochastics
*   RSI (Relative Strength Index)
*   Force Index

While multiple indicators can display divergence, this lesson focuses primarily on the **MACD indicator**. The principles, however, apply equally to Stochastics, RSI, and others.

### 4.1.2 Types of Divergence
For the purpose of this lesson, we focus on two primary types of divergence:
1.  **Bearish Divergence:** Used to anticipate a reversal from the top of an uptrend.
2.  **Bullish Divergence:** Used to anticipate a reversal from the bottom of a downtrend.

---

## 4.2 Bearish Divergence

Bearish divergence signals a potential reversal downward from an established uptrend. It is identified at the top of an uptrend when the price reaches a new high.

### 4.2.1 Identification Criteria
At the top of an uptrend, traders must determine whether the price will continue higher or reverse. Bearish divergence provides evidence of an impending reversal. It occurs when:
*   The price chart makes **higher highs** (new swing highs).
*   The indicator chart makes **lower highs** (lower swing highs).

This conflict indicates weakening upward momentum. As a counter-trend trader, this is the signal to sell short, anticipating a price decline. A stop loss must always be placed above the swing high to manage risk if the trend continues upward.

### 4.2.2 Rules for Connecting Swing Highs
To accurately identify bearish divergence, traders must follow strict rules when selecting and connecting swing highs:

1.  **Compare Swing Highs Only:** Always connect and compare swing highs on both the price chart and the indicator chart.
2.  **Non-Intersecting Price Line:** You may select any two swing highs, provided that a straight line connecting them does **not intersect any price action** between the two points. If the line crosses through price candles, the selection is invalid.
3.  **Vertical Alignment:** The two swing highs selected on the price chart must be vertically aligned with the two swing highs on the indicator chart. Exact alignment is not required; a deviation of one or two candles is acceptable, but the points must be substantially aligned. Misaligned points cannot be compared.
4.  **Non-Intersecting Indicator Line:** The line connecting the two swing highs on the indicator chart must also not intersect any other indicator peaks or lines between the two points.
5.  **Swing High Confirmation:** A swing high is only confirmed after the **next candle closes**. If the subsequent candle closes at a higher price, the previous point is no longer the swing high.
6.  **Entry Confirmation:** After a confirmed swing high, wait for the next candle to close as a **bearish candle** before entering a short position. If the next candle is bullish, wait for a bearish candle to appear to confirm downward momentum.

### 4.2.3 Example 1: General Motors (GM)
*   **Context:** Historical chart from a few years prior, at the top of an uptrend.
*   **Analysis:** Multiple swing highs were present. Two specific swing highs were selected because connecting alternative pairs would have violated the non-intersecting price line rule.
*   **Indicator:** MACD (black line).
*   **Alignment:** The MACD swing highs were vertically aligned with the price swing highs. Lines drawn on both charts did not intersect intervening data.
*   **Result:** Price made higher highs; MACD made lower highs. This confirmed bearish divergence, indicating a high probability of a downward reversal.

### 4.2.4 Example 2: Cabot (CBO)
*   **Context:** Top of an uptrend.
*   **Analysis:** A swing high was confirmed once the subsequent candle closed. Multiple previous swing highs existed. Two valid swing highs were connected without intersecting price action.
*   **Indicator:** MACD.
*   **Alignment:** The selected price swing highs corresponded vertically with MACD swing highs. Neither connecting line intersected intervening data.
*   **Result:** Price made higher highs; MACD made lower highs. Bearish divergence was confirmed.
*   **Additional Signal:** A **bearish pin bar** appeared at the swing high. Because a bearish pin bar is a reversal pattern, a short entry can be executed immediately after its formation, even before a full bearish candle confirmation.

## 4.3 Bullish Divergence

Bullish divergence signals a potential reversal upward from an established downtrend. It is identified at the bottom of a downtrend when the price reaches a new low.

### 4.3.1 Identification Criteria
At the bottom of a downtrend, traders must determine whether the price will continue lower or reverse. Bullish divergence provides evidence of an impending reversal. It occurs when:
*   The price chart makes **lower lows** (new swing lows).
*   The indicator chart makes **higher lows** (higher swing lows).

This indicates weakening downward momentum. As a counter-trend trader, this is the signal to buy (go long), anticipating a bounce or trend reversal.

### 4.3.2 Rules for Connecting Swing Lows
To accurately identify bullish divergence, traders must follow strict rules when selecting and connecting swing lows:

1.  **Compare Swing Lows Only:** Always connect and compare swing lows on both the price chart and the indicator chart.
2.  **Non-Intersecting Price Line:** You may select any two swing lows, provided that a straight line connecting them does **not intersect any price action** between the two points.
3.  **Vertical Alignment:** The two swing lows selected on the price chart must be vertically aligned with the two swing lows on the indicator chart. A deviation of one or two candles is acceptable.
4.  **Non-Intersecting Indicator Line:** The line connecting the two swing lows on the indicator chart must not intersect any other indicator troughs or lines between the two points.
5.  **Swing Low Confirmation:** A swing low is only confirmed after the **next candle closes with a higher low**.
6.  **Entry Confirmation:** Traders may enter immediately at the high of a bullish reversal pattern (e.g., bullish pin bar) or wait for a confirmed bullish candle. Waiting for confirmation is more conservative but results in a higher entry price.

### 4.3.3 Example 1: S&P 500 ETF (SPY)
*   **Context:** March timeframe, marking the bottom of the financial crisis/subprime mortgage crisis.
*   **Analysis:** Multiple swing lows were present. Two valid swing lows were connected without intersecting price action.
*   **Indicator:** MACD.
*   **Alignment:** MACD swing lows were vertically aligned with price swing lows.
*   **Result:** Price made lower lows; MACD made higher lows. Bullish divergence was confirmed.
*   **Additional Signal:** A pin bar appeared at the swing low. Confirmation occurred when the next candle closed with a higher low.
*   **Action:** Buy/go long to catch the reversal.

### 4.3.4 Example 2: Goldman Sachs (GS)
*   **Context:** 2015, bottom of a downtrend.
*   **Analysis:** Multiple swing lows existed. One potential pair was rejected because the MACD lacked a corresponding swing low for vertical alignment. A valid pair was selected where both price and MACD swing lows aligned.
*   **Result:** Price made lower lows; indicator made higher lows. Bullish divergence confirmed.
*   **Additional Signal:** A **bullish pin bar** appeared (red candle with a long lower shadow and small upper body).
*   **Action:** Buy immediately at the high of the pin bar, or wait for a bullish candle confirmation for a more conservative entry.

## 4.4 Practical Chart Analysis and Trade Execution

Divergence can appear on any timeframe. The following examples use daily candles and demonstrate multi-indicator analysis, precise trade setups, and execution.

### 4.4.1 Multi-Indicator Analysis Framework
When scanning for divergence, using multiple indicators increases conviction. In the examples below, three indicators are monitored simultaneously:
*   MACD
*   RSI
*   Stochastics

If divergence appears on two or more indicators, the signal is considered significantly more powerful.

### 4.4.2 Trade Setup 1: Bearish Divergence on Intel (INTC)
*   **Context:** Daily chart, top of an uptrend.
*   **Identification:**
    *   Price made higher highs. A new swing high was confirmed when the next candle made a lower high.
    *   **MACD:** Made significantly lower highs.
    *   **RSI:** Made lower highs.
    *   **Stochastics:** Flat (not significant).
    *   **Result:** Two out of three indicators showed divergence. Strong bearish signal.
*   **Trade Plan:**
    *   **Entry:** Sell stop order placed below the low of the confirming bearish candle.
        *   Candle Low: $44.10
        *   Entry Price: **$44.05**
    *   **Stop Loss:** Placed 3 cents above the swing high.
        *   Swing High: $45.18
        *   Stop Loss: **$45.21**
    *   **Risk (1R):** $45.21 - $44.05 = **$1.16 per share**
    *   **Profit Target:** 2R risk/reward ratio.
        *   2R = $1.16 × 2 = $2.32
        *   Target = $44.05 - $2.32 = **$41.73**
    *   **Context:** Trading against the uptrend. The 50-day moving average acts as support. The profit target ($41.73) aligns just at or above this support level.
*   **Outcome:** The next day, price declined, triggered the sell order, and hit the profit target. **Winning trade.**

### 4.4.3 Trade Setup 2: Second Bearish Divergence on Intel (INTC)
*   **Context:** Daily chart, subsequent new high at the top of an uptrend.
*   **Identification:**
    *   Swing high confirmed by a bearish candle close.
    *   Price made higher highs.
    *   **MACD:** Made much lower highs.
    *   **RSI:** Showed divergence.
    *   **Stochastics:** Flat.
    *   **Result:** Two out of three indicators showed divergence. Strong bearish signal.
*   **Trade Plan:**
    *   **Entry:** Sell stop order placed 3 cents below the candle low.
        *   Candle Low: $46.28
        *   Entry Price: **$46.25**
    *   **Stop Loss:** Placed 3 cents above the swing high.
        *   Swing High: $48.39
        *   Stop Loss: **$48.42**
    *   **Risk (1R):** $48.42 - $46.25 = **$2.17 per share**
    *   **Profit Target:** 2R risk/reward ratio.
        *   2R = $2.17 × 2 = $4.34
        *   Target = $46.25 - $4.34 = **$41.91**
    *   **Context:** 50-day moving average support nearby.
*   **Outcome:** The next day, price declined, triggered the sell order, and hit the profit target. **Winning trade.**

### 4.4.4 Trade Setup 3: Bullish Divergence on Cisco (CSCO) – First Attempt
*   **Context:** 1-year chart, bottom of a downtrend.
*   **Identification:**
    *   Price made lower lows.
    *   **MACD:** Made higher lows.
    *   **RSI:** Made higher lows (valid connections without intersection).
    *   **Result:** Two indicators showed divergence. Valid trade setup.
    *   **Candle:** Bullish pin bar. Confirmed when the next candle made a higher low.
*   **Trade Plan:**
    *   **Entry:** Buy order placed above the high of the candle.
        *   Candle High: $45.28
        *   Entry Price: **$45.28**
    *   **Stop Loss:** Placed 3 cents below the low.
        *   Candle Low: $44.09
        *   Stop Loss: **$44.06**
    *   **Risk (1R):** $45.28 - $44.06 = **$1.22 per share**
    *   **Profit Target:** 2R risk/reward ratio.
        *   2R = $1.22 × 2 = $2.44
        *   Target = $45.28 + $2.44 = **$47.72**
    *   **Caveat:** A large gap was present on the chart. Gap edges often act as strong resistance/support, which reduced the trade's attractiveness. However, the trade was taken for demonstration purposes.
*   **Outcome:** The next day, a bearish engulfing candle appeared. Price exceeded the entry high (triggering the buy order) but then reversed downward, hitting the stop loss. **Losing trade (-1R).**

### 4.4.5 Trade Setup 4: Second Bullish Divergence on Cisco (CSCO)
*   **Context:** Following the stop-out, a new swing low formed.
*   **Identification:**
    *   Divergence still existed.
    *   Price made lower lows.
    *   **MACD:** Continued making higher lows.
    *   **RSI:** Continued making higher lows.
    *   **Confirmation:** Next candle made a higher low. Traded waited for the first bullish candle to appear.
*   **Trade Plan:**
    *   **Entry:** Buy stop order placed 3 cents above the high of the bullish candle.
        *   Candle High: $43.83
        *   Entry Price: **$43.86**
    *   **Stop Loss:** Placed 3 cents below the swing low.
        *   Swing Low: $43.05
        *   Stop Loss: **$43.02**
    *   **Risk (1R):** $43.86 - $43.02 = **$0.84 per share**
    *   **Profit Target:** 2R risk/reward ratio.
        *   2R = $0.84 × 2 = $1.68
        *   Target = $43.86 + $1.68 = **$45.54**
    *   **Context:** Target positioned just below the recent swing high and the 50-day moving average.
*   **Outcome:** The next day, price rose, triggered the buy order, and hit the profit target. **Winning trade (+2R).**
*   **Net Result:** First trade lost 1R; second trade won 2R. Overall position remained profitable.

## 4.5 Risk Management and Trading Psychology

### 4.5.1 The Mathematics of Win Rate
No trading strategy is 100% accurate. Profitability is determined by risk management, not by being right every time.

*   **Risk/Reward Ratio:** Always risk 1R to make 2R (or more).
*   **50% Win Rate Scenario:**
    *   Assume 100 trades over a period.
    *   50 losing trades: 50 × -1R = **-50R**
    *   50 winning trades: 50 × +2R = **+100R**
    *   Net Profit: 100R - 50R = **+50R**
*   **Position Sizing:** If risking 1% of capital per trade (1R = 1%), a net profit of 50R equals a **50% return** on the portfolio.
*   **Key Principle:** Trading is a business. Winning trades are revenue; losing trades are costs. As long as winners exceed losers in magnitude, the strategy is profitable. Professional trading is not about being right all the time; it is about winning more when right than losing when wrong.

Divergence alone anticipates reversals but does not guarantee them. The subsequent lesson on the **Bollinger Mean Reversion** strategy will introduce additional filters to increase win rates, including:
*   Bollinger Bands integration.
*   Advanced price action analysis (identifying fakeouts and double tops that trigger stop losses before reversing).

## 4.6 Chapter Summary

*   **Divergence** is a conflict between price trend and indicator trend, used to anticipate reversals.
*   **Bearish Divergence:** Price makes higher highs, indicator makes lower highs. Signals a short opportunity at the top of an uptrend.
*   **Bullish Divergence:** Price makes lower lows, indicator makes higher lows. Signals a long opportunity at the bottom of a downtrend.
*   **Strict Rules Apply:** Swing points must be confirmed by candle closes, vertically aligned between price and indicator, and connected with lines that do not intersect intervening data.
*   **Execution:** Use sell stop orders for bearish divergence and buy stop orders for bullish divergence. Always define entry, stop loss, and profit target based on a 1:2 risk/reward ratio.
*   **Mindset:** Accept losses as part of the process. Consistent profitability comes from maintaining a positive risk/reward ratio over a series of trades, not from perfect prediction.
